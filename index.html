Tania Sales Suite: Live Sales Web App

A custom-built web dashboard (not Power BI) used daily by sales leadership. It shows hourly sales vs. yesterday, target and last year across Carton and Gallon business units, 13 channels and 6 regions, plus fleet KPIs like truck average and drop size.

ProblemSales results arrived as end-of-day SAP extracts, with no view of fleet productivity or target pace during the day.
SolutionAutomated SAP billing ingestion into the cloud, a cleaned and de-duplicated sales model, and a fast web app with 4 views: Dashboard, Analytics, Explorer and Trends.

  4. Business logicTruck avg (shared vs. unique truck-days), drop size = volume ÷ unique bills, net value after discount
  5. Web appHTML/JavaScript front end with SSO login, hourly refresh and 4 analysis views

SUPPLY CHAIN · CLIENT: TANIA WATER

Finished-Goods Stock Sufficiency (Days of Cover)

A Metabase dashboard that answers one question every morning: which branch will run out of which SKU, and when? It combines opening stock with average daily sales to compute days of cover (DOS), then ranks every region × SKU by urgency with a suggested replenishment.

ProblemStock and sales sat in different SAP reports, so stock-outs were discovered at the branch instead of prevented.
SolutionOne model of opening stock (unrestricted + in transit + QC) and a 14-trading-day sales average. DOS is colour-coded and comes with an expected stock-out date and replenishment to 14 days.
ImpactSupply planners get a ranked action list by region and SKU instead of reconciling spreadsheets.
How it's built
  1. SAP stock & billingStock by plant and storage location (unrestricted, in transit, QC) plus net sales
  2. Ingestion & storage in GCPDaily extracts landed and stored in Google Cloud
  3. Transform in GCP (SQL)Plant → region mapping, selling-unit conversion, 14-trading-day average excluding Fridays, Eids and outlier days, returns netted off
  4. DOS logicDOS = (Stock − ADS) ÷ ADS · urgency bands · stock-out date · replenishment = ADS × 14 − stock
  5. Metabase dashboard3 tabs with linked filters (as-at date, region, brand, SKU, urgency, days of cover)

Sales Budget vs. Actual — FY 2025/26

A management report that tracks every branch and channel against the annual sales budget. It covers value and units, month by month, and drills from main category to sub-category to individual product.

ProblemBudget follow-up lived in separate Excel files, so gaps showed up weeks late.
SolutionOne model that joins actual sales with the budget at branch × month × product level. Seven pages go from an executive overview down to SKU detail.
ImpactManagement sees under-performing branches and categories mid-month and can act before quarter-end.
How it's built
  1. Excel exportsSales transactions, budget sheet and product master exported from the ERP
  2. Power QueryCleans, unpivots and maps budget lines to product hierarchy and branches
  3. Star schema + DAXBudget and Sales facts share Date, Branch and Product dimensions. Measures cover achievement %, variance, top month and units vs. value
  4. Power BI report7 pages with slicers, conditional formatting and drill-down
  5. In progress: Python pipelineAutomates the extraction and outputs clean tables to Power BI, replacing the manual Excel exports

SALES OPERATIONS · CLIENT: ALSANIDI

Daily Sales Transactions

A near real-time view of daily sales across channels, retail branches and product categories, with weekday patterns, day-of-month trends and year-over-year comparison.

ProblemDaily sales figures came from manual ERP extracts, so management saw yesterday's numbers late and inconsistently.
SolutionAn automated pipeline pulls transactions over OData into a Fabric Dataflow Gen2, with incremental refresh so only new days are loaded.
ImpactThe report refreshes automatically every day with fast loads, so the sales team starts the day with up-to-date numbers.
How it's built
  1. OData feed (D365)Sales transactions read directly from Dynamics 365 through its OData endpoints
  2. Dataflow Gen2 (Fabric)Power Query transformations, data types and keys, landed into a Lakehouse/semantic model
  3. Incremental refreshRangeStart/RangeEnd partitions: only recent days reload, while history stays cached
  4. Scheduled automationDaily refresh with no manual steps
  5. Power BI reportChannel, branch, category, weekday and YoY analysis (Gregorian and Hijri calendars)

PURCHASING · CLIENT: ALSANIDI

Summer Season Purchase Order Analysis

A planning tool for the buying team. It checks a proposed seasonal purchase order against last season's sales, current stock and expected revenue before the order is approved.

ProblemSeasonal orders were sized by gut feeling, which caused both over-stock and lost sales.
SolutionCompare ordered units with last season's sales for each SKU, flag under-ordering, and estimate the sales lost before stock arrives.
ImpactOrder lines are approved, trimmed or increased based on data, with the revenue impact visible upfront.
How it's built
  1. D365 F&OSales history, on-hand stock and item master from Dynamics 365 Finance & Operations
  2. External sheetsThe proposed purchase order and cost sheet, maintained by the buying team in Excel
  3. Mixed (composite) modelERP tables and Excel sheets combined in one model through shared item keys
  4. DAX measuresCoverage %, gap vs. demand, lost sales before arrival, YoY difference
  5. Power BI reportOverview and comparison with last season
